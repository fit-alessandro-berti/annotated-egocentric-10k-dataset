\subsection{\textbf{RQ1}: Construction and structural properties}
\label{sec:structure}
Table~\ref{tab:corpus} reports the released corpus. All 10{,}698 events have the required values, nonnegative ordered endpoints, and labels in the relevant factory lists. Start times are nondecreasing within each log. There are no empty logs or zero-duration events. However, 35 adjacent event pairs overlap, 20 in factory \textbf{001} and 15 in factory \textbf{002}. Passing schema and vocabulary checks therefore does not imply a non-overlapping segmentation. Activity vocabulary use ranges from 89.3\% to 96.7\%.

\subsection{\textbf{RQ2}: Consistency with generated transcripts}
\label{sec:consistency}
The original parser finds 82.1\% of event boundaries within five seconds of a recognized generated transcript endpoint. It yields an overall coverage diagnostic of 52.8\%. Neither statistic is video accuracy. The coverage denominator is heavily affected by the literal multi-hour endpoints listed in Table~\ref{tab:anomalies}. Boundary agreement can also be low when formats change or when \textbf{S4} splits a described interval. These diagnostics alone do not establish extraction truncation.

\subsection{An end-to-end foundry example}
\label{sec:worked}
This example shows how a description of several actions becomes two labeled events with an inferred internal boundary. In factory \textbf{006}, worker 003, recording 00003, the generated transcript from \textbf{S1} describes actions from 00:04:05 to 00:04:36: placing an empty frame on a pattern plate, pouring and hand-packing powder, and using a wooden tool to level the surface. The worker summary from \textbf{S2} provides context by describing preparation, filling, packing, leveling, and compression of molds across recordings. The factory vocabulary from \textbf{S3} supplies process \emph{Sand mold creation} and activity \emph{level powder surface with wooden scraper}. The \textbf{S3} report also supplies a compact summary of this worker's molding work.

Event extraction in \textbf{S4} creates two intervals within that 31-second generated transcript entry: \emph{pack casting sand into mold frame}, from 00:04:05 to 00:04:24, and \emph{level powder surface with wooden scraper}, from 00:04:24 to 00:04:36. Audit event 41 judges the leveling event fully supported by its generated transcript. This supports the label's textual basis, not the exact internal split or a video-based duration measurement.

\subsubsection{Process flow and discovery}
\label{sec:discovery}
Video-as-case traces describe observation windows. Every recording has a distinct activity sequence in factories \textbf{001}, \textbf{004}, \textbf{005}, and \textbf{006}. Factory \textbf{002} has 53 distinct sequences across 54 recordings, and factory \textbf{003} has 70 across 72. Self-loops account for 4.3\% to 16.8\% of within-video directly-follows pairs. These statistics are reproduced directly from the stored labels. A self-loop can reflect separately extracted repetitions or segmentation choices; it is not a count of manufactured items.

The released discovery script uses Inductive Miner~\cite{leemans2013inductive} with noise threshold 0.2, video cases, and activity labels. Appendix~\ref{supp-app:discovery} of the Additional Material records the repeated calculation and current dependency version. The recalculated fitness ranges from 0.9544 to 0.9910 and precision from 0.0603 to 0.2679. Together, these results show that the discovered models can replay most behavior in the inferred logs while also permitting substantial additional behavior. Fitness measures how the discovered model can replay the inferred traces; precision measures how much additional behavior it permits relative to that log. Both are properties of a model-log pair, not accuracy against actual factory behavior.

\subsection{Six descriptive factory cases}
\label{sec:factorycases}
Each case below states its recorded scope, quantitative result, relationship to the report, and unresolved interpretation. Factory descriptions come from the generated reports; the logs quantify behavior described there. Different vocabularies, products, task mixes, and observation windows limit comparisons. The cases describe recorded work and motivate further study, but do not establish efficiency or the benefits of an intervention.

\paragraph{Factory \textbf{001}: hardware stamping and assembly.}
The archive has 97 clips from 11 workers; the time analysis retains 95 clips. Manual mechanical assembly accounts for 55.7\% of retained summed duration, metal stamping for 18.2\%, and component bagging for 11.3\%. The report describes material replenishment, floor storage, and operator transport. The 449 MH/TR rows and their short mean duration motivate examining how materials are supplied to workers. Product mix and compressed repetitions could also explain this pattern.

\paragraph{Factory \textbf{002}: garment finishing and packaging.}
There are 54 clips from ten workers, all retained by the event-end rule. Fabric folding, garment bagging, and ironing contribute 18.8\%, 17.1\%, and 13.2\% of summed duration. VA is 96.3\%. The report describes transport, floor staging, and varied folding methods. The small separate handling and transport share may leave those actions counted within other processes. Two anomalous generated transcript endpoints and mixed formats weaken temporal interpretation. Product-specific finishing requirements and category boundaries need closer examination.

\paragraph{Factory \textbf{003}: surface preparation and assembly.}
The archive covers 72 clips from 11 workers; all clips are retained. Surface finishing and polishing contributes 42.1\%, wax pattern assembly 19.2\%, and defect patching 10.8\%. The report identifies patching and material transport. The log quantifies patching's share without establishing defect rates or avoidable rework. Its 101 transport events total 1{,}230 seconds, yet the process-based TR share is zero because their durations enter other categories. Material types and quality criteria remain uncertain in the report itself.

\paragraph{Factory \textbf{004}: garment sewing and finishing.}
There are 51 clips from ten workers, all retained. Overlock seaming contributes 23.5\%, garment folding 11.0\%, and thread trimming 10.8\%. The median worker VA-transition rate is 10.6 per summed event-hour. The report describes tool switching, material handling, and inspection. The logs add a category-transition measure and a 24.3 percentage-point VA-share range in the two-worker ironing group. Product specifications, additional finishing duties, and recording windows could explain this difference.

\paragraph{Factory \textbf{005}: machining and motor assembly.}
The archive contains 22 clips from 11 workers, but only five workers meet the time threshold. All clips are retained, so the worker medians describe a smaller subset than the factory duration shares. Manual lathe machining contributes 32.1\%, drill press machining 19.1\%, and material transport and logistics 9.7\%. The report describes staging, fixturing, inspection, and coupled workstations. The log assigns 4.3\% to dimensional inspection. The two lathe profiles have similar VA shares, but their task equivalence is unknown. One generated transcript also has an unresolved endpoint anomaly.

\paragraph{Factory \textbf{006}: molding and foundry work.}
There are 26 clips from six workers, all retained; five workers meet the time threshold. Sand mold creation, pattern cleaning and coating, and compression molding contribute 17.4\%, 14.0\%, and 12.2\%. The report describes floor staging, material movement, cleaning, and mold-track operations. The logs assign 19.8\% of summed duration to MH/TR and yield a median worker mean VA run of 3.1 minutes. These quantities support closer study of handling requirements. Necessary mold preparation, differing materials, layout, and segmentation choices could all contribute. Two generated transcript endpoints require resolution against source metadata.

\subsection{From observations to operational hypotheses}
Table~\ref{tab:hypotheses} connects observations to explanations and the evidence needed to distinguish them. Reports identify phenomena; logs quantify them under a chosen representation. Differences in task mix, observation windows, and extraction choices remain competing explanations. Establishing causes or comparing efficiency requires matched operational evidence. Transport, inspection, cleaning, and documentation are not automatically waste, and their time is not automatically available for production. No intervention effect or cost saving is demonstrated by these descriptive results.
