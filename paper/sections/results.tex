\subsection{RQ2: Consistency with generated transcripts}
\label{sec:consistency}
The original parser finds 82.1\% of event boundaries within five seconds of a recognized transcript endpoint. It yields an overall coverage diagnostic of 52.8\%. Neither statistic is video accuracy. The coverage denominator is heavily affected by the literal multi-hour endpoints listed in Table~\ref{tab:anomalies}. Alignment can also be low when formats change or when S4 splits a narrated interval. We remove the previous interpretation of low coverage as demonstrated extraction truncation of long videos.

Partial-support examples show the difficulty of checking events against compressed text. Event sample 5 assigns a particular bagging activity where the transcript only states that repetitive assembly and packaging continue. Sample 40 assigns guiding an upper mold half where the text only says that the worker helps with a mold. Recall sample 10 uses a transcript interval from 11:51:00 to 12:59:00 in an anomalous file whose event log ends at 00:20:00. Its MISS judgment is preserved, but the original explanation as long-recording truncation is unverified. These cases show why a single accuracy label would obscure different sources of uncertainty.

\subsection{An end-to-end foundry example}
\label{sec:worked}
Consider factory 006, worker 003, recording \texttt{factory006\_worker003\_00003}. The S1 transcript interval from 00:04:05 to 00:04:36 describes placing an empty frame on a pattern plate, pouring and hand-packing powder, and using a wooden tool to level the surface. The S2 summary describes preparation, filling, packing, leveling, and compression of molds. It compresses many recordings into a worker-level account. The S3 report supplies process \emph{Sand mold creation}, activity \emph{level powder surface with wooden scraper}, and a compact summary of this worker's molding work.

S4 produces two events within that 31-second transcript interval: \emph{pack casting sand into mold frame}, from 00:04:05 to 00:04:24, and \emph{level powder surface with wooden scraper}, from 00:04:24 to 00:04:36. Both have process \emph{Sand mold creation}. Figure~\ref{fig:worked} shows what is retained and added. The internal boundary at 00:04:24 is inferred; S1 does not timestamp that transition separately. The events compress placement, pouring, and joint hand-packing into the first activity and add controlled names and a process context.

\subsubsection{Process flow and discovery}
\label{sec:discovery}
Video-as-case traces describe observation windows. Among the 97, 54, 72, 51, 22, and 26 videos in factories 001 to 006, there are respectively 97, 53, 70, 51, 22, and 26 distinct activity traces. Self-loops account for 4.3\% to 16.8\% of within-video directly-follows pairs. These statistics are reproduced directly from the stored labels. A self-loop can reflect separately extracted repetitions or segmentation choices; it is not a count of manufactured items.

The released discovery script uses Inductive Miner~\cite{leemans2013inductive} with noise threshold 0.2, video cases, and activity labels. Appendix~\ref{app:discovery} records the repeated calculation and current dependency version. The recalculated fitness ranges from 0.9544 to 0.9910 and precision from 0.0603 to 0.2679. Fitness measures how the discovered model can replay the inferred traces; precision measures how much additional behavior it permits relative to that log. Both are properties of a model-log pair, not accuracy against actual factory behavior.

Many distinct traces and low precision can arise from flexible work, recording boundaries, abstraction choices, label variation, or extraction mistakes. The present evidence does not separate these causes. No process-level discovery result is reported because its computation and saved output are unavailable. Replacing activity labels with process labels would change the represented behavior and evaluation target. A higher precision after that change would not by itself be a controlled demonstration of better extraction or modeling. No new process-level models are generated for this revision.

\subsection{Six descriptive factory cases}
\label{sec:factorycases}
Each case below states its recorded scope, quantitative result, relationship to the report, and unresolved interpretation. Factory descriptions are derived from the reports. Cross-factory rankings are limited by different vocabularies, products, and observation windows.

\paragraph{Factory 001: hardware stamping and assembly.}
The archive has 97 clips from 11 workers; the time analysis retains 95 clips. Manual mechanical assembly accounts for 55.7\% of retained summed duration, metal stamping for 18.2\%, and component bagging for 11.3\%. These differ from the earlier unfiltered dominant-process table because the two excluded logs are in this factory. The report already describes material replenishment, floor storage, and operator transport. The 449 MH/TR rows and their short mean duration quantify the assigned category pattern. They motivate examining material presentation, but do not establish that dedicated handlers, shorter travel, or a different layout would improve output. Product mix and compressed repetitions remain competing explanations.

\paragraph{Factory 002: garment finishing and packaging.}
There are 54 clips from ten workers, all retained by the event-end rule. Fabric folding, garment bagging, and ironing contribute 18.8\%, 17.1\%, and 13.2\% of summed duration. VA is 96.3\%. The report already describes transport, floor staging, and varied folding methods. Quantification shows a small separately categorized handling and transport share, not proof that those behaviors are rare within other processes. Two anomalous transcript endpoints and mixed formats weaken temporal interpretation. Product-specific finishing requirements and category boundaries need examination before any claim of superior efficiency.

\paragraph{Factory 003: surface preparation and assembly.}
The archive covers 72 clips from 11 workers; all clips are retained. Surface finishing and polishing contributes 42.1\%, wax pattern assembly 19.2\%, and defect patching 10.8\%. The report already identifies patching and material transport. The log quantifies patching's share without establishing defect rates, avoidable rework, or financial loss. It also records 101 transport events totaling 1{,}230 seconds even though the process-based TR share is zero. This case demonstrates how aggregation can hide an extracted behavior. Material types and quality criteria remain uncertain in the report itself.

\paragraph{Factory 004: garment sewing and finishing.}
There are 51 clips from ten workers, all retained. Overlock seaming contributes 23.5\%, garment folding 11.0\%, and thread trimming 10.8\%. The median worker VA-transition rate is 10.6 per summed event-hour. The report already describes tool switching, material handling, and inspection. The logs add a reproducible category pattern and a 24.3 percentage-point VA-share range in the two-worker ironing group. Product specifications, additional finishing duties, and recording windows could explain this difference. The data do not identify which changes to tools or stations would help.

\paragraph{Factory 005: machining and motor assembly.}
The archive contains 22 clips from 11 workers, with only five workers meeting the time threshold. All clips are retained. Manual lathe machining contributes 32.1\%, drill press machining 19.1\%, and material transport and logistics 9.7\%. The report describes staging, fixturing, inspection, and coupled workstations. The log adds duration shares, including 4.3\% assigned to dimensional inspection. The two lathe profiles have similar VA shares, but their task equivalence is unknown. Inspection can be necessary, and one transcript has an unresolved endpoint anomaly. The recorded shares cannot measure excess quality work or recoverable machine capacity.

\paragraph{Factory 006: molding and foundry work.}
There are 26 clips from six workers, all retained; five workers meet the time threshold. Sand mold creation, pattern cleaning and coating, and compression molding contribute 17.4\%, 14.0\%, and 12.2\%. The report already describes floor staging, material movement, cleaning, and mold-track operations. The logs assign 19.8\% of summed duration to MH/TR and yield a median worker mean VA run of 3.1 minutes. These quantities support a closer study of handling requirements, not a diagnosis of layout as their cause. Necessary mold preparation, differing materials, and segmentation choices are alternatives. Two transcript endpoints require resolution against source metadata.

Reports identify phenomena; logs can quantify them under a chosen representation. Explaining their causes requires additional evidence. Transport, inspection, cleaning, and documentation are not automatically waste, and the time assigned to them is not automatically available for production. No intervention effect or cost saving is demonstrated by these descriptive results.
