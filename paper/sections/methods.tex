\section{Assessment Methodology}
\label{sec:methods}
The assessment examines the structure of the extracted events, their consistency with generated descriptions, and their use in the reported descriptive analyses. It combines structural checks, the existing author audit, and calculations with explicitly defined measures.

The inputs are frozen texts, reports, CSVs, and archived author judgments. The descriptive workflow runs offline with Python's standard library. Activity discovery uses PM4Py~\cite{berti2023pm4py} with the archived miner and noise-threshold settings. All calculations use the released records as their input.

\subsection{Evaluation questions and reference data}
Table~\ref{tab:evaluation_scope} distinguishes the objects of evaluation. Assembly101 provides video action annotations~\cite{sener2022assembly101}; Seeth et al. compare text-derived logs with source traces and process-level references~\cite{seeth2026finetuned}. Our generated transcript checks assess an intermediate transformation, while our downstream calculations demonstrate uses of its output. These reference structures support different conclusions, so their accuracy, fitness, and precision values should not be ranked as if they measured the same property.

\subsection{Metrics, denominators, and aggregation}
\label{sec:metrics}
The measures describe different properties of the inferred logs. We therefore specify what each measure counts, which observations enter its denominator, and how results are combined across recordings or workers.

The coverage diagnostic measures how much of the parsed timestamp span is occupied by at least one extracted event. For each video, let $E_v$ be its events and $B_v$ the set of generated transcript endpoints recognized by the original parser. Let $T_v^{\max}$ be the maximum such endpoint and $M_v$ the maximum event endpoint. We first calculate occupied time and the endpoint span for each recording, then divide their factory totals:
\begin{equation}
 C_v=\left|\bigcup_{e\in E_v}[t_s,t_e)\right|,\qquad S_v=\max(T_v^{\max},M_v),\qquad
 \mathrm{coverage}_f=\frac{\sum_{v\in f}C_v}{\sum_{v\in f}S_v}.
\end{equation}
$C_v$ measures the seconds occupied by events, counting overlapping intervals only once. The vertical bars around the union of intervals mean its duration in seconds. $S_v$ measures the span from zero to the latest recognized generated transcript or event endpoint. The factory diagnostic divides total occupied seconds by total endpoint span. Recordings with longer endpoint spans contribute more to the result, so the ratio is weighted by span rather than an average of recording percentages. Its denominator comes from recorded endpoints, not verified footage durations. When no generated transcript endpoint is recognized, the event endpoint supplies the denominator. Results from the broader parser are released as a separate diagnostic; the original table is retained and labeled.

Boundary agreement measures how often an event's start or end lies within five seconds of a recognized generated transcript endpoint. This five-second tolerance is the matching rule in Table~\ref{tab:metrics}. Each event contributes two boundaries to the denominator. If no generated transcript endpoint is recognized for a recording, its event boundaries count as unmatched and remain in the denominator. Several event boundaries may match the same generated transcript endpoint. A match need not identify the correct action or justify an internal split.

Category shares describe how the retained event duration is distributed across the categories in the chosen process map. Let $E_f^*$ contain the factory's events retained for time analysis under $M_v\leq3600$, and let $E_w^*$ be the retained events for worker $w$. For each category, we sum the durations of events assigned to it and divide by the summed duration of all retained events. The process label determines the category. Each event row contributes its duration, including time that overlaps another row. Coverage instead counts that shared time only once.

Worker summaries use the same retained events. Worker event-hours $H_w$ come from summed event durations, converted from seconds to hours. We calculate each eligible worker's VA share first and then take the factory median, giving workers equal weight. For grouping, the dominant process is the one with the largest summed duration; subsequent worker statistics use all of that worker's retained events. In Table~\ref{tab:metrics}, $\#$ counts the indicated events or list elements, the indicator $\mathbf{1}$ equals one when its condition is satisfied and zero otherwise, and $\arg\max$ selects the process with the greatest summed duration.

Table~\ref{tab:metrics} defines the measures and their denominators. For category analysis, the process lists have 113 factory-specific entries and 108 distinct strings across factories. Of the 113 entries, 112 are used in the logs. The unused factory \textbf{005} label \emph{motor assembly finishing} has no category assignment in the original map. Every process string actually assigned to an event is mapped. Appendix~\ref{supp-app:mapping} of the Additional Material lists the map by factory and marks the unused entry explicitly.

The categories are value-adding production work (VA), material handling and staging (MH), transport (TR), quality inspection (QI), cleaning and maintenance (CM), rework (RW), and documentation (DOC). VA includes packaging and other completion work under the authors' scheme; it is not a direct measurement of customer value. Defect patching is assigned RW because its label and descriptions concern filling or repairing surface defects. This does not establish that it is avoidable or that the upstream process failed a specified quality standard. Supporting work can be necessary even when assigned outside VA.

Consecutive events with the same category form a run within each recording. A run's duration is the sum of its event durations, which can bridge unrepresented gaps and count overlaps. We divide run durations in seconds by 60 to express them in minutes. We calculate the mean VA-run duration for each eligible worker and then report the median of those worker means. No eligible worker has an empty VA-run list in this release; the original implementation would report zero for that case.

The worker's run sequences are concatenated, and changes from VA to another category are counted and divided by the worker's summed event-hours. This can count transitions across a clip join; the release contains 15 such transitions among eligible workers. The rates depend on the event representation and do not verify interruptions of continuous work. The original script rounds each worker's VA share, transition rate, and mean VA-run duration to one decimal before taking factory medians. We retain this rounding rule.

All per-video logs are nonempty. Negative and zero durations are checked separately; no such rows occur. Overlaps remain in time composition and are merged only for coverage. Ambiguous generated transcript timestamps remain literal. This preserves the original calculations while making their limits visible.

\subsection{Existing semantic audit protocol}
\label{sec:audit}
The audit asks two questions. The event sample checks whether labels are supported by overlapping generated text. The generated transcript-entry sample checks whether sampled entries overlap extracted events. The archive records 42 event-support judgments and 42 transcript-entry-overlap judgments, seven of each per factory. One author made the judgments. The audit record documents a random seed of 42 and factory stratification, but the complete sampling implementation is unavailable. The sampling order, full sampling frame, and handling of timestamp formats cannot be reconstructed. We report the existing sample as documented and do not claim that it can be redrawn exactly.

For the event sample, the reference is the generated text overlapping the event interval. Code C means fully supported: the label describes the action in that interval's generated transcript. P means partially supported: the label is plausible but the overlapping text alone is insufficient. X means contradicted. Of the 42 sampled events, 36 are fully supported and six are partially supported. Dividing the fully supported count by all sampled events gives strict support of $36/42=0.857$. The six P cases remain in the denominator, so this rate measures full textual support.

For the generated transcript-entry sample, COV denotes overlap with at least one event, EXC an uncovered entry judged non-operational, and MISS an uncovered entry judged operational. There are 34 COV entries, four MISS entries, and four EXC entries. The reported calculation retains the four uncovered operational entries and excludes only the four uncovered non-operational entries. Dividing the overlapping entries by that retained total gives $34/(34+4)=0.895$. We call this the \emph{reported entry-overlap rate}, not action recall. One COV entry describes camera tilt covered by a longer event. Thus, the denominator does not establish 38 independently verified operational entries, and overlap does not show that all actions or labels were extracted correctly.

The audit measures consistency with generated transcripts. It cannot identify visual errors, missed actions, or mistaken object interpretations already present in the generated transcripts. There is no independent evaluator, inter-rater agreement estimate, or source-video audit. The sample proportions do not bound error in downstream time shares, category assignments, observation selection, or causal interpretation. Appendix~\ref{supp-app:audit} of the Additional Material reproduces all judgments and their notes, with the unverified truncation explanation clearly marked.
