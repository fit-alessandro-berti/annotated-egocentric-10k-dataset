\section{Assessment Methodology}
\label{sec:methods}
The assessment uses frozen texts, reports, CSVs, and archived author judgments. The descriptive workflow runs offline with Python's standard library. Activity discovery uses PM4Py~\cite{berti2023pm4py} with the archived miner and noise-threshold settings. All calculations use the released records as their input.

\subsection{Evaluation questions and reference data}
Table~\ref{tab:evaluation_scope} distinguishes the objects of evaluation. Assembly101 provides video action annotations~\cite{sener2022assembly101}; Seeth et al. compare text-derived logs with source traces and process-level references~\cite{seeth2026finetuned}. Our transcript checks assess an intermediate transformation, while our downstream calculations demonstrate uses of its output. These reference structures support different conclusions, so their accuracy, fitness, and precision values should not be ranked as if they measured the same property.

\subsection{Metrics, denominators, and aggregation}
\label{sec:metrics}
For each video, let $E_v$ be its events and $B_v$ the set of transcript endpoints recognized by the original parser. Let $T_v^{\max}$ be the maximum such endpoint and $M_v$ the maximum event endpoint. The original coverage diagnostic uses
\begin{equation}
 C_v=\left|\bigcup_{e\in E_v}[t_s,t_e)\right|,\qquad S_v=\max(T_v^{\max},M_v),\qquad
 \mathrm{coverage}_f=\frac{\sum_{v\in f}C_v}{\sum_{v\in f}S_v}.
\end{equation}
The denominator begins at zero. This is an endpoint-span ratio, weighted by span, not a mean of clip ratios and not footage coverage. When no transcript endpoint is recognized, the event endpoint supplies the denominator. The corrected parser's results are released as a separate diagnostic; the original table is retained and labeled.

A boundary matches if its distance from \emph{any} recognized transcript endpoint is at most five seconds. Both ends of every event enter the denominator, including events in files with no recognized transcript entries. Multiple event boundaries can match the same transcript boundary. A match need not identify the correct action or justify an internal split.

Table~\ref{tab:metrics} defines the measures and their denominators. For category analysis, the process lists have 113 factory-specific entries and 108 distinct strings across factories. Of the 113 entries, 112 are used in the logs. The unused factory \textbf{005} label \emph{motor assembly finishing} has no category assignment in the original map. Every process string actually assigned to an event is mapped. Appendix~\ref{supp-app:mapping} of the Additional Material lists the map by factory and marks the unused entry explicitly.

The categories are value-adding production work (VA), material handling and staging (MH), transport (TR), quality inspection (QI), cleaning and maintenance (CM), rework (RW), and documentation (DOC). VA includes packaging and other completion work under the authors' scheme; it is not a direct measurement of customer value. Defect patching is assigned RW because its label and descriptions concern filling or repairing surface defects. This does not establish that it is avoidable or that the upstream process failed a specified quality standard. Supporting work can be necessary even when assigned outside VA.

Runs collapse consecutive same-category rows \emph{within each video}. Their duration is the sum of member durations, which can bridge unrepresented gaps and count overlaps. Run lists are then concatenated across a worker's clips before VA-to-nonVA transitions are counted. This can count transitions across a clip join; the release contains 15 such transitions among eligible workers. These rates are therefore representation-dependent transition measures, not verified interruptions of continuous work. The original script rounds each worker's VA share, transition rate, and mean run duration to one decimal before taking factory medians. We retain this rounding rule. No eligible worker has an empty VA-run list in this release; the original implementation would report zero for that case.

All per-video logs are nonempty. Negative and zero durations are checked separately; no such rows occur. Overlaps remain in time composition and are merged only for coverage. Ambiguous transcript timestamps remain literal. This preserves the original calculations while making their limits visible.

\subsection{Existing semantic audit protocol}
\label{sec:audit}
The archive records 42 sampled events and 42 sampled transcript entries, seven of each per factory. One author made the judgments. The audit record documents a random seed of 42 and factory stratification, but the complete sampling implementation is unavailable. The sampling order, full sampling frame, and handling of timestamp formats cannot be reconstructed. We report the existing sample as documented and do not claim that it can be redrawn exactly.

For the event sample, the reference is the generated text overlapping the event interval. Code C means fully supported: the label describes the transcribed action in that interval. P means partially supported: the label is plausible but the overlapping text alone is insufficient. X means contradicted. Strict support is $36/42=0.857$. The six P cases are retained in the denominator, not treated as fully correct.

For the transcript-entry sample, COV denotes overlap with at least one event, EXC an uncovered entry judged non-operational, and MISS an uncovered entry judged operational. The archive reports $34/(34+4)=0.895$ after excluding four EXC entries. We call this the \emph{reported entry-overlap rate}, not action recall. One COV entry describes camera tilt covered by a longer event. Thus, the denominator does not establish 38 independently verified operational entries, and overlap does not show that all actions or labels were extracted correctly.

The audit measures consistency with transcripts. It cannot identify visual errors, missed actions, or mistaken object interpretations already present in the transcripts. There is no independent evaluator, inter-rater agreement estimate, or source-video audit. The sample proportions do not bound error in downstream time shares, category assignments, observation selection, or causal interpretation. Appendix~\ref{supp-app:audit} of the Additional Material reproduces all judgments and their notes, with the unverified truncation explanation clearly marked.
