\section{Measurement and Analytical Implications}
\label{sec:measurement}
\subsection{Interpreting a zero}
\label{sec:zeros}
A zero share does not prove that a behavior was absent. The recording may omit it, the model may miss it, or the category map may count it elsewhere. A suitable activity label alone does not ensure detection.

In factory \textbf{003}, the logs contain 101 transport events totaling 1{,}230 seconds, yet the transport (TR) share is zero. Categories are assigned by process, and none of this factory's processes maps to TR. The transport time is therefore counted under other categories. Interpreting a zero requires checking the recording scope, extracted events, and category rule.

Current records identify the source file and local time interval. They do not link each event to its supporting transcript entry or explain inferred boundaries, so checking an event requires manually matching intervals. Explicit links, boundary notes, and annotations of known data issues would make review easier~\cite{goel2022quality}. Alternative labels or time ranges could express uncertainty~\cite{pegoraro2019uncertain,pegoraro2021uncertain}, but would need supporting evidence. The five-second matching tolerance is not a guarantee of boundary accuracy.

Source durations and selection records are unavailable, and error rates have not been measured for each stage. Structural checks therefore cannot establish video accuracy or completeness.

\subsection{Choosing how to aggregate events}
A factory duration share weights events by their duration. A median worker share gives each eligible worker equal weight. These summaries answer different questions, as factory \textbf{006} shows (Table~\ref{tab:value}).

Event counts depend on segmentation; runs group consecutive events of the same category; interval unions count overlapping time once. Activity and process categories can also give different results. An analysis should state its unit, category rule, denominator, case definition, and treatment of gaps and overlaps before interpreting a result.
