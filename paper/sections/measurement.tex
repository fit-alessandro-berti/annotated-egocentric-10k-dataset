A zero in an analytical table has several possible causes (Table~\ref{tab:zeros}). These causes occur at different stages and require different checks. Requiring a label for transport or waiting improves representability, but does not establish reliable detection or a measured absence. A model may still omit the action, assign another label, or absorb it into a longer episode.

In factory \textbf{003}, both \emph{transport loaded tray to storage rack} and \emph{transport bulk material via wheelbarrow} are permitted activity labels and occur in the logs. Together they account for 101 events and 1{,}230 seconds. No process label is mapped to TR in that factory. Their durations therefore inherit categories such as VA from their assigned processes. The TR category's zero describes the map's output, not the absence of transport or the inability of the activity vocabulary to express it.

To interpret an observed zero as evidence of absence within a window, an analyst would need a defined observation scope, a suitable representation, adequate detection, correct assignment, and an aggregation rule that preserves the behavior. None follows from label membership alone. Conversely, an observed event can establish what the representation contains while leaving the event's visual accuracy unmeasured.

\FloatBarrier
Errors can propagate along both branches of Figure~\ref{fig:dependency}. A mistaken object description can shape the worker summary, the factory vocabulary, and the final event label. Agreement among those outputs can therefore reflect a shared error. Quality-aware construction~\cite{andrews2020quality} and event-log imperfection patterns~\cite{suriadi2017imperfections} establish the broader concern. Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty} shows how it arises in this construction, separating observed examples from possible mechanisms.

These mechanisms occur within an observation scope that is itself incomplete: the selection protocol and source durations are unavailable. No stage-wise error rates are calibrated, and structural checks cannot quantify visual errors. Worker and factory differences may therefore reflect observation selection as well as representational choices.

Readable text is useful evidence, but it is weaker than structured event provenance. Existing records identify a source file and local interval. They do not store supporting transcript-span identifiers, alternative labels, calibrated confidence, or a flag that distinguishes an inferred internal boundary from a copied transcript endpoint. Finding the source of an event therefore requires reading and matching intervals. The foundry example can be traced manually, yet its CSV does not record why the split at 00:04:24 was chosen.

A future schema could add transcript-entry references, a boundary-origin field, and vocabulary and prompt versions. Data-quality annotations could retain known issues for downstream analysis~\cite{goel2022quality}. Explicit alternative labels or bounded times would instead extend the event representation toward uncertain data~\cite{pegoraro2019uncertain,pegoraro2021uncertain}. Such alternatives need evidence: the five-second matching tolerance does not imply that an event boundary lies within five seconds of its true value. Calibrated confidence values are unavailable for the existing events. Provenance can explain how a conclusion was reached; it does not certify that every preceding observation was correct.

\subsection{Missing time does not imply a one-sided bound}
\label{sec:missingtime}
The observed share outside VA is sometimes described as a lower bound because waiting and other supporting work can be omitted. That interpretation also assumes that omitted time is sufficiently non-VA. Missing VA time can instead raise the observed non-VA share.

\paragraph{Proposition.}
Consider an idealized observation with $C>0$ seconds of correctly classified, non-overlapping observed time, of which $N$ seconds are non-VA. Let $U\geq0$ seconds be unobserved. If observed and unobserved time partition a known target window, then its full-time non-VA share satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac{N}{C+U}\ \leq\ p_{\mathrm{nonVA}}\ \leq\ \frac{N+U}{C+U}.
\label{eq:missing}
\end{equation}
\emph{Proof.} Let $x$ be the non-VA duration in the unobserved part. Since $0\leq x\leq U$, the full share is $(N+x)/(C+U)$ and has the stated extrema. Both are attainable: all missing time may be VA or all may be non-VA.

For $U>0$, the observed ratio $N/C$ is a lower bound only if $x/U\geq N/C$. Thus, merely knowing that some waiting is missing is insufficient. The interval width in Equation~\ref{eq:missing} is $U/(C+U)$, which shows how unknown coverage limits a share even with perfect observed labels.

This is a conceptual result, not an empirical confidence interval for this dataset. Source durations and therefore $U$ are unavailable; labels are inferred; and summed event durations include overlaps. Substituting the coverage diagnostic or the 97.1 retained summed event-hours into this proposition would violate its assumptions. The result identifies the additional evidence needed before making a bound claim.

\subsection{Why aggregation changes the question}
A duration-weighted factory share estimates the composition of the retained event rows. A median worker share gives equal weight to each eligible worker. They answer different questions. In factory \textbf{006}, for example, the factory VA share is 74.2\%, while the median worker VA share is 87.0\%. Different observation amounts and work profiles can produce this difference without either statistic being erroneous.

Likewise, process-based and activity-based categories need not agree. Counting rows emphasizes the extraction granularity; counting runs suppresses adjacent labels; measuring interval unions removes overlap but does not resolve classification conflicts. None is a universal correction. A defensible analysis states its unit, denominator, case rule, handling of gaps, and interpretation before drawing operational conclusions. The released measurements serve as a reproducible example of those choices and their limits.
