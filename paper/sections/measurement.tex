\section{Measurement and Analytical Implications}
\label{sec:measurement}
\subsection{Observability and the interpretation of zeros}
\label{sec:zeros}
A zero in an analytical table has several possible causes (Table~\ref{tab:zeros}). These causes occur at different stages and require different checks. Requiring a label for transport or waiting improves representability, but does not establish reliable detection or a measured absence. A model may still omit the action, assign another label, or absorb it into a longer episode.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\caption{Possible meanings of an apparent zero, ordered along the evidence path. Only the last mechanism is directly demonstrated by the factory 003 example; the others are possible mechanisms implied by the pipeline.}
\label{tab:zeros}\footnotesize
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.38\linewidth}LL@{}}\toprule
Mechanism & Where information is lost & Needed check \\\midrule
Behavior absent from the recorded window & Observation selection & Define the window and verify that it is suitable for the question \\
Behavior present but not narrated & Video interpretation or narration & Independent review of source footage \\
Behavior narrated but lost in summaries & S2 or S3 compression & Compare transcripts with worker and factory summaries \\
No suitable vocabulary label & Vocabulary induction & Inspect lists against the behavior of interest \\
Suitable label exists but is not assigned & Event extraction & Compare relevant transcript spans and extracted labels \\
Event exists but category aggregation hides it & Analytical mapping & Compare activity-level rows with process-derived categories \\
\bottomrule\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

In factory 003, both \emph{transport loaded tray to storage rack} and \emph{transport bulk material via wheelbarrow} are permitted activity labels and occur in the logs. Together they account for 101 events and 1{,}230 seconds. No process label is mapped to TR in that factory. Their durations therefore inherit categories such as VA from their assigned processes. The TR category's zero describes the map's output, not the absence of transport or the inability of the activity vocabulary to express it.

To interpret an observed zero as evidence of absence within a window, an analyst would need a defined observation scope, a suitable representation, adequate detection, correct assignment, and an aggregation rule that preserves the behavior. None follows from label membership alone. Conversely, an observed event can establish what the representation contains while leaving the event's visual accuracy unmeasured.

\subsection{Uncertainty propagation and limits of provenance}
\label{sec:uncertainty}
Errors can propagate along both branches of Figure~\ref{fig:dependency}. A mistaken object description can shape the worker summary, the factory vocabulary, and the final event label. Agreement among those outputs can therefore reflect a shared error. Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty} links each error source to an existing check and the conclusion it can affect.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\caption{Error sources, existing checks, and analytical consequences. No calibrated stage-wise error rates are available.}
\label{tab:uncertainty}\footnotesize
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.22\linewidth}LL@{}}\toprule
Error source & Existing check and remaining gap & Possible analytical consequence \\\midrule
Visual interpretation & S1 asks for uncertainty; no independent video audit & Incorrect tools, materials, or actions enter all later outputs \\
Narration and repetition & Text can be inspected; no exhaustive action coverage check & Waiting or cycles disappear; episode counts change \\
Worker/factory summarization & Nonempty outputs and report parsing; no information-retention measure & Rare behavior does not influence the vocabulary \\
Vocabulary induction & Permitted lists are inspectable; completeness and compatibility are unchecked & No suitable label, or a forced approximate label \\
Event labeling & Membership checks and 42 archived judgments against text & A valid label can still describe the wrong action \\
Boundary estimation & Offset parsing, end-before-start checks, and transcript alignment; no source-duration check & Biased durations, overlap, or misleading endpoint spans \\
Case construction & Source files and offsets retained; real inter-clip order and gaps unknown & Artificial directly-follows relations and synthetic elapsed time \\
Analytical categorization & Public process map; no independent value classification & Supporting actions can be hidden inside VA contexts \\

\bottomrule\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Readable text is useful evidence, but it is weaker than structured event provenance. Existing records identify a source file and local interval. They do not store supporting transcript-span identifiers, alternative labels, calibrated confidence, or a flag that distinguishes an inferred internal boundary from a copied transcript endpoint. Finding the source of an event therefore requires reading and matching intervals. The foundry example can be traced manually, yet its CSV does not record why the split at 00:04:24 was chosen.

A future schema could add transcript-entry references, a boundary-origin field, vocabulary and prompt versions, alternative labels, and explicit uncertainty types~\cite{pegoraro2019uncertain}. Such fields would need evidence and validation. We do not retrofit invented confidence values to the existing events. Provenance can explain how a conclusion was reached; it does not certify that every preceding observation was correct.

\subsection{Missing time does not imply a one-sided bound}
\label{sec:missingtime}
The observed share outside VA is sometimes described as a lower bound because waiting and other supporting work can be omitted. That interpretation also assumes that omitted time is sufficiently non-VA. Missing VA time can instead raise the observed non-VA share.

\paragraph{Proposition.}
Consider an idealized observation with $C>0$ seconds of correctly classified, non-overlapping observed time, of which $N$ seconds are non-VA. Let $U\geq0$ seconds be unobserved. If observed and unobserved time partition a known target window, then its full-time non-VA share satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac{N}{C+U}\ \leq\ p_{\mathrm{nonVA}}\ \leq\ \frac{N+U}{C+U}.
\label{eq:missing}
\end{equation}
\emph{Proof.} Let $x$ be the non-VA duration in the unobserved part. Since $0\leq x\leq U$, the full share is $(N+x)/(C+U)$ and has the stated extrema. Both are attainable: all missing time may be VA or all may be non-VA.

For $U>0$, the observed ratio $N/C$ is a lower bound only if $x/U\geq N/C$. Thus, merely knowing that some waiting is missing is insufficient. The interval width in Equation~\ref{eq:missing} is $U/(C+U)$, which shows how unknown coverage limits a share even with perfect observed labels.

This is a conceptual result, not an empirical confidence interval for this dataset. Source durations and therefore $U$ are unavailable; labels are inferred; and summed event durations include overlaps. Substituting the coverage diagnostic or the 97.1 retained summed event-hours into this proposition would violate its assumptions. The result identifies the additional evidence needed before making a bound claim.

\subsection{Why aggregation changes the question}
A duration-weighted factory share estimates the composition of the retained event rows. A median worker share gives equal weight to each eligible worker. They answer different questions. In factory 006, for example, the factory VA share is 74.2\%, while the median worker VA share is 87.0\%. Different observation amounts and work profiles can produce this difference without either statistic being erroneous.

Likewise, process-based and activity-based categories need not agree. Counting rows emphasizes the extraction granularity; counting runs suppresses adjacent labels; measuring interval unions removes overlap but does not resolve classification conflicts. None is a universal correction. A defensible analysis states its unit, denominator, case rule, handling of gaps, and interpretation before drawing operational conclusions. The released measurements serve as a reproducible example of those choices and their limits.
