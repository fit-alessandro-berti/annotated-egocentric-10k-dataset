\abstract{Much of manual factory work remains invisible to information systems: moving materials, preparing tools, checking surfaces, and repairing defects can leave no event record. Egocentric video brings this work into view, but turning what a camera sees into a process log requires decisions about actions, names, and time. We present a construction method and linked data resource that make these decisions inspectable. A vision-language model produces timestamped descriptions of visible work, which we call generated transcripts. Pretrained models summarize these descriptions, derive shared factory labels, and extract labeled event intervals. The resulting logs support exploration of work composition, local sequences, and differences between recorded worker profiles. Worked examples show how the representation shapes the findings. Transport remains in the event log, but its duration is counted under other categories because the category rule uses broader process labels. A described episode can also acquire an additional event boundary during extraction. Structural checks and an author audit assess the records and their consistency with generated transcripts, while accuracy against the source videos remains an open question. Preserving the intermediate descriptions alongside the logs lets researchers trace measurements to their textual basis and investigate alternative representations of manual work.}
