\abstract{Manual factory work often leaves few records in information systems. We present a method and data resource for constructing event logs from egocentric video and interpreting the resulting measurements. Five stages produce transcripts, worker summaries, factory vocabularies, interval events, and merged logs. The released artifacts contain 322 per-video logs with 10{,}698 events from 59 workers in six factories. Extraction uses no task-specific training or manual input annotations. All released events have the required fields and permitted labels, but these properties do not establish accuracy against video. An existing author audit finds full transcript support for 36 of 42 sampled events, partial support for six, and no contradictions. We formalize the representation, document temporal inconsistencies, and reproduce the existing descriptive calculations from frozen outputs. These calculations assign 10.8\% of retained summed event duration in one factory to defect patching, a behavior already described in its report. They also show why a zero transport category can coexist with 101 recorded transport events: categories are assigned from process context. Worked examples, metric definitions, and an analysis of missing time explain what these logs can and cannot establish. The resource supports inspection and hypothesis generation; video accuracy, causal diagnoses, and intervention effects remain unmeasured.}
